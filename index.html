1. Introduction to Teamwork (W1L8)

The video: penguins, ants and crabs

  • Each group faces a predator. Alone they panic. Together they defeat the enemy.
  • What they show: presence of mind, quick thinking, playing to their strengths, and perfect coordination and connection while working toward a common goal.
  • "Together everyone achieves more", which is what teamwork is about.

Professor's lessons from the video

  • Fear is human. Everyone gets scared, and the cause is uncertainty about the outcome.
  • ★ In the face of danger or uncertainty: do NOT panic. Show restraint. That is how you gain control of yourself.
  • On their own, people react according to their personality. Together, the force is much greater.
  • What the three videos have in common: seemingly weak creatures came together and overcame a powerful enemy.

★ Two real-world examples of collective power

Example What happened
Employee / labour unions (after the Industrial Revolution) Factory workers were denied basic rights and amenities, and owners were profit-focused. Workers formed unions with union leaders as their voice to management. The result was a "mini revolution". It was not a direct fight. It was raising issues of just cause with management.
Diversity & Inclusion (D&I) (last ~2 decades) People talked about accommodating different cultures and genders, but workplace systems and policies did not support it. Leaders came together, formed interest groups, and kept communicating. This led to policy changes across the board. D&I is now part of most top corporates.

2. What is Teamwork? (W1L9.1): The Geese Lesson ★★

★ Formal definition (from the slides)

"Teamwork is defined as collaboration by a group of people to achieve a common purpose. Teamwork is often a feature of day-to-day working, and is increasingly used to accomplish specific projects, in which case it may bring together people from different functions, departments, or disciplines. A team should ideally consist of people with complementary skills." (Teamwork (2011). In Business: The Ultimate Resource. A&C Black.)

★ Geese flying in a V-formation: 5 facts and the lesson behind each

# What the geese do Teamwork lesson
1 Each goose creates uplift for the goose behind it, so the whole flock flies more efficiently Working as a team is more efficient than working alone
2 A goose that leaves the V immediately has to work harder to keep up Moving toward the same destination together takes less individual effort
3 Leadership rotates. A tired lead goose drops back and another takes the front Shared leadership spreads the burden and improves efficiency
4 Geese at the back honk to encourage the leader to keep its speed Communication with the whole team matters, and encouraging/positive feedback promotes success
5 If a goose gets sick and falls out, two others stay with it until it recovers and can join another flock Empathy and care for team members beats a selfish approach. "A loss to the flock is a loss for all"
  • Key phrase: shared goals and shared leadership.
  • Professor's figure: the V-formation reduces effort by roughly 30–60% (he said he wasn't sure of the exact number).
  • Nobody teaches the geese this. It is inborn. Everyone shoulders the responsibility equally, which is how they fly across continents.

Teamwork in nature brings ORDER, BALANCE and HARMONY

Examples given: galaxies, stellar systems and the solar system; atoms → molecules → elements and compounds → materials; single cells → multicellular organisms; cells → tissues → organs → organ systems. ★ "Together we achieve order, harmony and balance in every aspect of life."

3. Why Teamwork is Important and Relevant (W1L9.2)

★ Slide list: 10 benefits of teamwork

  1. Builds competitive advantage
  2. Saves time, energy, costs and resources
  3. Covers competency and skill gaps
  4. Improves workforce efficiency and productivity
  5. Inspires ideation, creativity & innovation
  6. Builds higher morale
  7. Peer learning and self-improvement
  8. Creates synergy
  9. Enhances workplace experience
  10. Balances needs & goals of the organization (and individual)

Lecture explanation (the chain of reasoning)

  • A team draws on a diverse set of competencies, skills and abilities. Members complement each other and create positive synergy.
  • Synergy → generation of ideas → creativity and innovation.
  • Higher morale comes from encouraging, cheering, learning from, and even challenging one another, which brings out each person's best.
  • We lead ourselves AND share leadership. Shared goals lead to improved communication, healthy conflict, and better workplace efficiency.
  • All of this creates a harmonious, positive workplace. That means a better experience, higher job satisfaction, happiness and fulfilment, and savings in time, energy, cost and resources. The result is "co-creation and pure magic".

★ Ratan Tata quote

"If you want to walk fast, walk alone. But if you want to walk long, walk together."

  • Why: no task is accomplished by a single individual. You need people of different kinds blending in sync.
  • The leader's job is to blend different strengths and give people the right roles and responsibilities so they work in tandem.

★ Example: the account leader aligning strengths with roles

  • Problem: many mid-level managers had the same managerial responsibilities. Nobody enjoyed the work, and team output was poor.
  • What they did: held one-on-one meetings during annual performance appraisals to find what brought out each person's best.
  • Three patterns emerged:
    1. People who wanted creative / innovative work
    2. People strong with metrics/numbers but uncomfortable dealing with people (reporting to management, etc.)
    3. People strong in people relationships (inside the team and with external teams) but uncomfortable with numbers
  • Fix: aligned each person's strengths with their role as far as possible.
  • Result: people enjoyed their work, became more comfortable, expressive and "shining", and the team output became very effective.

5. ★★★ The 10 C's of Teamwork (ORDER MATTERS)

# C Core idea in one line
1 Clarity Vision, purpose and values plus clear roles. "The most primary, deserves to be first"
2 Commitment Personal, internal honour code. Turn expectations into clear agreements
3 Communication "At the core of teamwork." Safe space, psychological safety, standard processes
4 Competence Diverse, complementary skills. Right person, right role (NASCAR pit crew)
5 Collaboration Divergent thinking, psychological safety, shared leadership (Sundar Pichai)
6 Coordination Convergent thinking: plan, schedule, execute (Mumbai dabbawalas)
7 Connection Getting along: respect, trust, empathy, celebration (Dr. Abdul Kalam story)
8 Conflict Resolution Source of conflict = expectations. Dialogue, active listening, win-win
9 Change Management Plan → initiate → control → stabilize. Expect resistance
10 Continuous Improvement Kaizen, 1% per day, tiny steps, feedback loops

Mnemonic: "Clear Commitment Comes from Competent Collaborators Coordinating, Connecting, Conflict-resolving, Changing, Continuously improving."

1

Clarity

Lecture

  • The organisation's vision, purpose and core values must be clearly defined and communicated to teams regularly.
  • This helps members align and balance their own needs, goals and values with the organisation's.
  • Members see the big picture: how their role contributes, and how it makes the world better. Their work feels worthwhile and important.
  • ★ Clarity inspires: shared vision, ownership, intrinsic motivation, commitment, which lead to greater job satisfaction, which leads to higher retention rates.
  • It helps you choose the right role and the right organisation, and pick the right people for your team. It helps leaders, team members AND the organisation: "it works in all ways."

Slide

  • Align and balance organization, team and individual in terms of: Vision, Purpose, Mission, Core Values
  • Clear and well-defined:
    • Roles & responsibilities with accountability
    • Individual and shared goals & objectives
    • Rules, policies, standards, processes

Professor's points:

  • ★ Clarity is "very very primary" and "deserves the position of being first." It applies to an individual as well as a team. A team can't succeed unless each individual is clear about themselves.
  • Army example: in war the front stretches for miles with thousands of soldiers. Orders and roles must be clear and passed down the hierarchy to the last man. The last soldier on the ground must know what to do in different circumstances.
  • ★ ABC of Communication: A = Accuracy, B = Brevity, C = Clarity
  • ★ Story: mobile left on the hillock (the helper and the cycle)
    • A friend left his mobile on the breakfast table at the top of a hill and asked a restaurant helper, "Can you go have a look?"
    • He said "take my cycle". The helper couldn't ride, so he pushed it uphill. It took 15 minutes instead of about 3.
    • Then the helper came back without the phone: "You told me to look at the mobile. I saw it, it's there. You didn't tell me to get it."
    • Lesson 1: You need clarity about your own role and about your teammate's position/abilities (you didn't know he couldn't cycle). "Understand each other's position."
    • Lesson 2: Give clear, complete instructions. "Like algorithms: do this, then this; if yes do this, if not do that."
2

Commitment

Lecture

  • ★ Cricket example: one player stops the ball at the boundary at the last moment, and two others back him up with collection and throwing. Together they save one run. "Every run counts."
  • Commitment to the game, to work, to excellence starts with personal internal commitment to oneself. That makes you go the extra mile and give your best.
  • ★ What inspires commitment?
    • Shared vision, shared goals
    • Well-defined roles and responsibilities: who does what, when, why, with whom, where
    • Service Level Agreements (SLAs) when a client is involved (formal agreements)
  • Inside teams there usually aren't clear agreements. The lead expects things from members, members from the lead, and members from each other. So expectations and intentions should be turned into clear mutual agreements that define outcomes, timelines and deliverables.
    • This reduces or eliminates misunderstandings, assumptions and ambiguity, which boosts productivity, performance and commitment.
  • Value team members and their contribution, like geese honking to encourage each other and strengthen their collective commitment.
  • ★ The maid example: she agreed to tell the instructor by the previous night if she wasn't coming. Once she couldn't get through and made ~100 missed calls. There was no written agreement, yet she kept her commitment. She was referred to others as someone you could trust and rely on, even if her work wasn't 100% perfect.
  • ★ Simon Sinek video: being there for one another and trust are basic tenets of teamwork.

Slide

  • Clearly defined roles and responsibilities, shared vision
  • SLAs (Service Level Agreements)
  • Convert expectations & intentions into clear (written) agreements, which give clear outcomes, timelines, deliverables
  • ★ "Commitment is an Honor Code, it is to yourself. It is 'Personal and Internal'"
  • Commitment makes you go the extra mile to put in your best
  • Value team members and their contribution to inspire commitment

Professor: ★ Armed forces story (the section commander)

  • A section commander (in charge of 10 soldiers) sees one of his men dying in a trench 10–15 m away during a fierce battle.
  • He radios his platoon commander, who says no: leaving would open a gap and a vulnerability, and "I can't lose you too."
  • Out of commitment to his comrade, he crawls over anyway. The soldier dies in his lap.
  • When asked why he risked it: "*My presence felt so good to him that he breathed his last easily. His last words were, 'Sir, I knew you will come.'*"
  • Lesson: commitment to your people/colleagues, and the trust that comes from it.

Professor: ★ Definition and deep vs surface commitment

  • ★ "Commitment is a deep intent to make sure things happen the way we envision."
  • Plan the micro-level details: who does what, deadlines, resources needed, time required, contingencies if things go wrong.
  • Surface-level commitment: "I will do the project."
  • Deep commitment: When do we meet next? What will we discuss? Who owns each action item? Who tracks what doesn't go to plan? There is constant focus on execution.
  • Commitment means being fully involved, watching for obstacles, keeping people in sync, and making progress.
  • ★ Words of commitment: date, time, contingency, backup, plan of action.
  • ★ NOT commitment: vague words like "it will be done" or "we will do it" with no date or time. Commitment does not assume everything goes to plan. It asks how to mitigate if it doesn't.
3

Communication

Lecture

  • ★ "Communication is at the core of teamwork."
  • Teams have diverse people: introverts, extroverts, ambiverts, listeners, speakers, and people who struggle with language or communication skills.
  • Create a safe, non-judgmental space for everyone to share.
  • Informal space breaks the ice, helps people get to know each other, and lets them express themselves freely. It also builds social, interaction, communication and language skills.
    • ★ Example: TGIF ("Thank God It's Friday"). Every Friday the project team met for informal events, games, pranks, and short presentations on passions, hobbies and new technologies. This made it easier to speak up in formal meetings.
  • Formal space must be welcoming, encouraging and involving, so people ask questions, share opinions, take part in dialogue, listen and collaborate.
  • Knowledge sharing: build a knowledge repository / bank of best practices, templates, guidelines, processes and lessons learned. Share it within and across teams. This indirectly builds commitment and intrinsic motivation, because people learn and also share.
  • ★ Fundamental principle: whether formal or informal, communication must be empowered, open, honest, transparent, with mutual respect, trust and empathy. This is called creating PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY in the workplace.
  • Clear, streamlined processes, templates and guidelines make formal communication smoother and raise productivity. Examples: how to run meetings, minutes-of-meeting templates, internal emails, formal emails to clients.
  • Large organisations usually have standards (documented or available for reference). When you join, spend time studying and observing these processes and standards.
  • Small organisations may not have them, so take the initiative to set them up for everyone's benefit.

Slide

  • Create space to ask questions and give answers
  • Share ideas, experiences, and best practices
  • Empower through open, honest, transparent interaction
  • Clear guidelines for email, messages, meetings, Minutes of Meeting (MOM), internal and external communication
  • Streamlined process for feedback, escalation, appraisals, follow-ups and timelines

Competence

Lecture

  • A team has diverse competencies to maximise each person's potential, strengths, capabilities and skills, and to bridge and cover skill gaps.
  • ★ Types of competencies: technical, functional, domain-specific, leadership, behavioural, managerial, soft skills, life skills.
  • Complementary strengths, talents, abilities and skills lead to learning from each other, which improves creativity and innovation.
  • Like the geese: each member leads him/herself by taking ownership and responsibility for shared goals, and contributes to collective goals as a team player, not as an individual.
  • ★ Shared vision + shared leadership + shared goals lead to effective collaboration, cooperation and coordination.
  • ★ NASCAR pit crew video (changing four tyres): a team of diverse competencies working toward a common goal.

    "The right person, with the right competencies, in the right role, at the right place, performing the right work, in the right way, and that is pure magic."

Slide

  • ★ "Core competence is getting along, working together in harmony towards a shared goal and shared vision"
  • Diverse competencies to bridge gaps
  • Complementary strengths, talents, abilities and skills
  • Lead oneself by taking responsibility, initiative and ownership
  • Contribute like a team member (not as an individual)
  • NASCAR slide text: the pit crew works toward the common goal of winning the race. Their plan is to work at top speed with no errors while checking the car, fixing parts, changing tyres and pumping gas. Each member has a specific job and a clear sense of what to do, and each keeps fine-tuning their job to shave time off pit stops, which can win or lose a race.

Collaboration

Lecture

  • Collaborative teamwork builds a huge competitive advantage. It brings out the best in creativity, innovation, productivity and performance.
  • ★ Leaders act as FACILITATORS and create PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY to build TRUST.
  • ★ Psychological safety = creating a safe, non-judgmental space and environment.
  • Psychological safety is essential for DIVERGENT thinking. It activates the team's diverse backgrounds and skills. Members get freedom and independence to explore, share and exchange ideas.
  • ★ Power goes to shared leadership, shared vision, shared goals, shared creation and shared innovation, NOT to authority or hierarchy.
  • Equal access to resources, equal opportunity to participate, speak, share and present.
  • Risk-taking is allowed. People are not afraid of mistakes or failure.
  • Open, honest, transparent communication is key. Icebreakers, games and informal events build engagement and involvement.
  • ★ Levels of collaboration:
    • Within a team
    • Across teams (horizontal and vertical in the organisation)
    • With clients, service providers, partners
  • Collaboration across teams and locations is helped by collaborative tools and well-defined, streamlined processes.

Slide

  • Generation of ideas by activating a team's collective talents and skills
  • Equal access to opportunities and resources
  • Exchange of creative and innovative ideas (irrespective and independent of authority and hierarchy)
  • Facilitate shared leadership toward common goals
  • Collaborative tools and streamlined processes for shared tasks across locations and teams

Professor: ★ Sundar Pichai (Google CEO)

  • He is highly competent technically, but that is not what made him CEO. Google has plenty of top technologists and architects.
  • ★ The biggest ability a CEO needs is getting people to work together. Experts are attached to their own viewpoints.
  • A leader balances: listens to every viewpoint, creates coordination, and drives the group to a CONSENSUS.
  • ★ "A leader is finally a person amongst equals": someone who can get along, listen empathetically, and build consensus.

Coordination

Lecture

  • ★ Coordination FOLLOWS collaboration.
    • Collaboration = DIVERGENT thinking (getting ideas on the table)
    • Coordination = CONVERGENT thinking (narrow down, pick the idea that works for everyone and meets the criteria, then implement it)
  • It is about details, planning, scheduling, execution, and identifying coordination points: tasks, timelines, deliverables, who does what, who needs what, how, when, where.
  • Clear visibility of your own and others' progress and direction is vital.
  • Peer cooperation is key: peers assist and support each other.
  • ★ Mumbai Dabbawalas case study:
    • ~5,000 dabbawalas serve ~2 lakh (200,000) Mumbaikars with home-cooked food
    • 100% customer satisfaction, 99.9999% performance
    • A case study for clarity, commitment, coordination and cooperation
    • ★ For coordination to work at its best, you need clarity, commitment, effective communication and competence.

Slide

  • Coordination is all about convergent thinking
  • Converge on one or two ideas, then plan, schedule, execute
  • Integrate execution plans
  • Visibility on each other's progress and direction
  • Assist and support peers

Professor

  • Activities students did: the A-frame march and the caterpillar walk.
  • The event itself needed coordination among many stakeholders: the IIT Madras BS Degree studio team, the Dean of Students' office, faculty, students and assistants. Their synchronisation and coordination made it a success.

Connection

Lecture

  • ★ Connection = getting along with one another in harmony and peace.
  • Teams work together for long periods, so they need mutual respect, trust and understanding. This is the foundation for open, honest, transparent communication, which leads to effective collaboration and coordination and inspires commitment.
  • Like the geese: cheer and motivate each other, and care for one another in times of need. Support each other through thick and thin.
  • ★ Empathy, caring and guidance transform the workplace. This comes from:
    • Valuing individuals and their contribution
    • Acknowledging and appreciating milestones and achievements
    • Celebrating in authentic, spontaneous and unique ways (also covered in the Leadership Lessons module)

Slide

  • Formal team building to build self-worth, self-trust and mutual trust
  • Informal get-togethers
  • Cultivate empathy and caring through good and bad times
  • Offer guidance and support to one another
  • Acknowledge and appreciate milestones and successes

Professor: ★★ Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam story (trust and empathy)

  • Point: build trust by understanding each member's personal and professional needs.
  • A scientist on an important project asks his boss to leave at 5:30 pm to take his children to an exhibition. The request is unusual, so the boss realises it must matter a lot and says yes.
  • The scientist gets absorbed in work and only finishes around 8 pm. He goes home feeling guilty about his kids and his wife.
  • At home his wife is calm and gives him coffee: "Your manager came and took the kids to the exhibition. He'll drop them back in 10–15 minutes."
  • The boss was Dr. Abdul Kalam.
  • ★ Lesson: a good leader takes care of the project and the integrity of the team member's family. "Unless you are integrated and happy as a family unit, you cannot really be productive in the office." It shows forethought, experience and empathy.

Conflict Resolution

Lecture

  • Conflict will happen: between team members, between the lead and the team, or with the client.
  • ★ Psychological safety is very important for conflict resolution because it "sets the space."
  • Prevention: a safe, non-judgmental space with open, transparent, honest, empathetic communication, where everyone is valued and there is good connection. This goes a long way in preventing conflict.
  • ★ When conflict happens: have a dialogue, listen actively to understand what is going on and the underlying root causes, and look for WIN-WIN solutions.
  • Main goal: keep a peaceful, harmonious and trusting workplace.

Slide

  • Clear rules and guidelines for different types and severities of conflict
  • Well-defined escalation and resolution mechanism
  • Effective communication and listening
  • Build preventive and proactive strategies

Professor

  • ★ "If there is no conflict between employees, there must be something seriously wrong." Conflict is normal.
  • ★ The SOURCE of every conflict is EXPECTATION. Conflict comes from a gap between expectation and the outcome achieved.
  • Interpersonal conflict (between people) is different from inner conflict (inside yourself: should I do this or avoid it?).
  • ★ "ABC" strategy people use to escape tasks (this is NOT the ABC of communication):
    • A = Avoid the task you don't want to do
    • B = Bypass: if the boss watches closely, you work around it, do something else and look busy
    • C = Confuse: if you can't avoid or bypass, you muddle things and create conflict somewhere, which then never ends
  • Remedy: be clear about your role, accept your part as a responsible member, and contribute to the end result.
  • For interpersonal, inter-team or inter-group conflict, it can be resolved if someone intervenes who looks rationally at the arguments for and against, keeping the organisation's health in mind.
  • ★ Resolution comes from: understanding each other's roles, give and take, and putting your own or your group's interest below the organisation's overall growth and objective.

Change Management

Lecture

  • ★ "Change is constant." The instructor's company reorganised every year. The first time felt like the world shifting. Over time they adapted faster and even started to look forward to it.
  • ★ Types and sources of change at the workplace:
    • Reorganisations
    • Mergers, acquisitions and integrations (some smooth and friendly, some not; resistance and resentment are common)
    • Strategy changes (a strategy makeover with cascading impact)
    • Market changes (new competitors, new markets)
    • Leadership/manager changes (new people join, old ones leave)
    • Role changes (promotion or a different role, with new objectives and growth)
    • Workflow, process and project changes (projects can last years, months or weeks)
    • Location changes (within the organisation, offsite ↔ onsite), new client / new culture / new work
    • Global events: COVID crisis, 9/11 attack (2001) (the instructor lost two colleagues in the World Trade Centre), 2008 financial crisis
    • Personal change (good or bad). It affects the individual and also their team members, sometimes the whole team.
  • How team members cope: work with leads, managers and the organisation so the change goes smoothly. Take initiative and responsibility, participate actively, share feedback, ask questions, keep communication constant and flowing, take inputs, support each other.
  • ★ A well-defined, documented change-management strategy helps a lot. It must be customisable and adaptable to the type of change, and it gives direction and guidance.
  • ★ When implementing a change strategy, remember:
    • Expect RESISTANCE. Resistance to change can turn into resentment.
    • Communicate openly and transparently to reduce or eliminate misunderstandings, tension, anxiety and misinformation.
    • Listen actively, ask questions, take feedback. This clarifies issues and changing priorities.
    • Share the big picture and direction at regular intervals.
    • Use team members' individual talent and competence to manage the change.
    • Empower them with decision-making.
    • Keep moving forward, with tiny steps and big steps. Making progress is the key.

Slide

  • ★ Definition: "Change management means to PLAN, INITIATE, CONTROL and then finally STABILIZE the change processes on both organizational and personnel levels."
  • ★ Two perspectives:
    1. Organizational change management: top-level, managerial, for the overall organisation
    2. Individual change management: lower-level, employee transition
  • Training and development to empower team members
  • Set goals and objectives so it's clear how everyone's role contributes to the bigger picture
  • Engage team members and manage resistance to change

Continuous Improvement

Lecture

  • A culture of continuous improvement in deliverables, products and processes improves quality and efficiency, and so customer service and customer happiness.
  • It also boosts morale and commitment, and self-worth as people realise their potential.
  • ★ Continuous improvement = making tiny changes every day. Their compound effect over time gives massive results.
  • ★ James Clear, Atomic Habits: 1% improvement every day compounds. The lecture said "a 37 percent change over a year". (Exam note: the book's actual claim is 1.01^365 ≈ 37 times better. If an MCQ quotes the lecture, the number is 37.)
  • It creates a continuous cycle of learning, improvising and growing, which is good for creation and innovation.
  • ★ Lean and iterative processes with comprehensive feedback loops make course corrections easier.
  • How to implement it: collaborative ideation, idea generation and brainstorming sessions to find improvements, fine-tune processes and improve quality.
  • Because change is constant, keep upgrading your skills and abilities. This builds an ADAPTIVE culture.

Slide

  • Course correction with comprehensive feedback loops
  • Learn, improvise, iterate and grow
  • Adaptive to change
  • Workshops and team trainings to upgrade competencies and skills
  • Lean and iterative processes
  • Tiny steps and tiny gains
  • Surveys and assessments

Professor: ★★ KAIZEN

  • The Japanese were pioneers of continuous improvement.
  • ★ KAIZEN: "Kai" = change, "Zen" = better. It means continuously making things better.
  • Everyone from the CEO to the most junior employee contributes to organisational and process improvement.
  • Classic example: the auto industry, which reached close to zero defects.
  • Joke: a buyer asked a Japanese supplier for fewer than a certain number of defects. The Japanese supplier didn't understand what a "defect" was, so they added one deliberately and sent it.
  • Why it matters: with so many market and internal changes, big problems can't be solved overnight. Break them into small pieces, assign the right people, and keep improving one day / one week at a time to get drastic improvement over time.

Professor: ★★ Fortune 500 client case study (continuous improvement in practice)

  • Context: a software vendor for a Fortune 500 client. The client suddenly saw problems with employee attrition, knowledge management, and lack of innovation.
  • There was no overnight fix. They formed core groups, assigned the right people to keep thinking about each problem, and tried small improvements.
  • Attrition: the employee-satisfaction team ran a survey on what made people unhappy. Answers included: a different technology, job rotations, breaks / less stressful projects, fun Fridays. They listened and acted on each one.
  • This fed into productivity, and then into higher customer satisfaction.
  • Timeline: 1 to 1.5 years (not overnight).
  • ★ Results:
    • Employee Satisfaction Index: 3.6 → 4.1 (out of 5) in about one year
    • Customer Satisfaction Index: 3.9 → 4.2

Key pairs and contrasts (common MCQ traps)

  • Collaboration = DIVERGENT thinking vs Coordination = CONVERGENT thinking. Coordination follows collaboration.
  • ABC of communication (Accuracy, Brevity, Clarity) vs ABC of escaping tasks (Avoid, Bypass, Confuse).
  • Surface commitment ("I will do it") vs deep commitment (dates, owners, tracking, contingencies).
  • Organizational change management (top-level) vs Individual change management (employee transition).
  • Interpersonal conflict vs inner conflict.
  • SLAs = formal agreements with clients. Inside teams, turn expectations into clear (written) mutual agreements.
  • Commitment is personal and internal, an honor code to yourself.
  • Core competence (per the slide) = getting along / working in harmony toward a shared goal.
  • Psychological safety = a safe, non-judgmental space. The leader acts as facilitator to create it. It appears in Communication, Collaboration and Conflict Resolution.
  • Collaboration power goes to shared leadership/vision/goals, not to authority or hierarchy.
  • Source of conflict = expectations. "No conflict at all means something is seriously wrong."
  • Clarity is first because it is the most primary.
  • Kaizen: Kai = change, Zen = better.

One-line definitions

  • Teamwork: collaboration by a group of people to achieve a common purpose, ideally with complementary skills.
  • Commitment: a deep intent to make sure things happen the way we envision.
  • Psychological safety: a safe, non-judgmental space and environment.
  • Connection: getting along with one another in harmony and peace.
  • Coordination: convergent thinking. Converge on an idea, then plan, schedule, execute.
  • Change management: plan, initiate, control and stabilise change processes at organisational and personnel levels.
  • Continuous improvement: tiny daily changes whose compound effect gives massive results.

8. Possible Long-Answer Question

"What are some of the challenges associated with working in a team? Explain with a real-life example." Answer frame:

  1. Define teamwork (the Business: The Ultimate Resource definition).
  2. Name 3–4 challenges from the list of 9 (e.g. lack of trust, working in silos, lack of common goals, not managing the weakest link).
  3. Give a real example (a college project or a workplace).
  4. Map each fix to a C: lack of direction → Clarity; lack of trust → Connection/Communication; conflict → Conflict Resolution; silos → Collaboration/Coordination; weakest link → Competence, peer support, Continuous Improvement.
  5. Close with a quote (Ratan Tata or Jim Stovall).
